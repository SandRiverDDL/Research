# Synthesis and Ideas

Use this for comparison tables, research maps, survey-style summaries, idea generation, novelty-risk checks, and reviewer mode.

## Evidence Stack

Read compact durable context first:

1. existing reports or comparison files
2. generated research map / idea context if present
3. relevant L1 cards
4. bounded paper or survey briefs
5. original source sections only when needed for a strong claim

Do not concatenate every card or full PDF into context by default.

## Reports

Reports should add cross-paper judgment, not repeat card fields.

A strong report should identify:

- the field's core tension
- method families and lineage
- decision axes that change the problem definition
- evidence credibility
- contradictions or incomparable evaluations
- open questions and idea hooks
- evidence used beyond existing cards

If a report uses new evidence not already captured in cards, persist it in an `Evidence used` appendix or an L2 note.

## Idea Review

For each idea, pressure-test:

- Does the problem still matter in two years?
- Can the metric be gamed?
- Are baselines strong and fair?
- Is there evaluator, oracle-state, teacher-trace, or data leakage?
- Does the idea survive stronger frontier models?
- Is it more than an incremental delta?
- What would a skeptical reviewer reject?

End with the smallest change that would make the idea more credible.

## Novelty-Risk Checks

Look for direct competitors first, then adjacent methods that share the same failure mode, objective, or evaluation claim. Treat missing direct competitors as a blocker for confident novelty claims.

When uncertain, record the uncertainty as an open question rather than forcing a yes/no novelty verdict.
