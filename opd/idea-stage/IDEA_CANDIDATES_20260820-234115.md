# OPD Idea Candidate Pool — 2026-08-20

## Scope and hard exclusions

This pool was generated from three independent lenses over the local `notes/` corpus and the August 2026 OPD frontier. Existing idea documents were used only as an exclusion baseline.

Excluded families: training-step irreversibility localization; pre-training teacher–student distillability prediction; teacher intervention, handoff, or exit; trust/reliability controllers; entropy/KL/position/token/turn reweighting; rollout truncation or depth scheduling; multi-granularity routing; negative-trajectory counterfactual weighting; privilege taxonomies or benchmarks; representation-assisted trust; API-rubric process credit; contract-aware reward extrapolation; asynchronous/stale OPD.

## Evidence anchors

- [Towards Understanding OPD through the Lens of Test-Time Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11829): OPD improves average sampling efficiency, but at large K the base model can recover or surpass it; the reported forgotten set is larger than the newly solved set.
- [On-Policy Delta Distillation for Multilingual Math Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05802): teacher-minus-teacher-base deltas improve multilingual OPD, while English-only training can improve Korean/Japanese accuracy but shift outputs toward English.
- [On-Policy Replay for Continual Supervised Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29495): current-policy replay on historical prompts strongly reduces continual-SFT forgetting and is an important overlap for all replay ideas.
- [Invariant Gradient Alignment for Robust Reasoning Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05025): aligns update directions across logically isomorphic examples, creating direct novelty pressure for cross-language gradient-intersection ideas.
- [Revisiting On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25562): explicitly studies the bias–variance tradeoff between token-level OPD and sequence-level reverse KL, creating novelty pressure for sequence-level objectives.

## Mechanical merge and gate log

- Raw candidates: 19 (7 contradiction lens, 7 objective lens, 5 transfer/interference lens).
- Merged exact/near duplicates: `Sampler-Envelope` + `Decoder-Envelope`; `Matched-Delta` + `Matched-Ancestry`.
- Excluded before jury: `Prefix Transplantation Test`, because it is too close to the user's explicit ban on “which step becomes unrecoverable” causal localization.
- Unique candidates reaching jury: 16.
- Compute gate: all pilots can be run within one week of wall-clock time on a modest multi-GPU allocation; no candidate was removed on compute alone.

## Candidates

### C01 — Decoder-Envelope Capability Audit

**Claim.** A nontrivial part of OPD's apparent large-K capability loss may be mismatch between the post-OPD probability geometry and a single fixed decoder, not disappearance of correct trajectories.

**Method.** Prespecify a held-out grid over temperature, top-p, and min-p; give base, OPD, and off-policy controls the same total generated-token budget; compare fixed-decoder and envelope-level learned/retained/forgotten problem sets.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** One public 1.5B–1.7B base/OPD pair, 100–200 verifiable math problems, 6–8 decoders, 32–64 samples per decoder; bootstrap problem transitions. Estimated 4–50 A100-hours, 1–2 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** Test-time-scaling evaluation and compute-aligned training already argue for deployment-matched evaluation; novelty must come from changing the OPD capability-boundary conclusion rather than merely sweeping temperatures.

### C02 — Capability-Ledger Constrained OPD

**Claim.** The small-K/large-K tradeoff is not intrinsic: one can preserve pre-OPD verified solution modes as functional constraints while optimizing ordinary OPD elsewhere.

**Method.** Before training, sample and group verified base-model solution strategies. During OPD, monitor each group's likelihood and project updates that cross a predeclared floor back into the feasible set. Compare vanilla OPD, simple replay, and constrained OPD.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** Ledger from 256 prompts × 32 base samples; 100-step 1.5B student/7B teacher runs; pass@1, pass@64, and strategy-level retained/learned/forgotten accounting. Estimated 24–60 A100-hours, 2–4 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** On-policy replay and continual-learning retention methods already address forgetting; the distinct burden is showing that trajectory-level functional constraints preserve nearby capability rather than memorizing witnesses.

### C03 — Rare-Solution Replay OPD

**Claim.** Uniformly replaying rare verified base solutions alongside OPD can retain the base capability boundary without sacrificing sampling-efficiency gains.

**Method.** Archive low-frequency correct base trajectories before OPD and interleave them as ordinary supervised batches during matched-budget OPD.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** 100–200 prompts, 64–128 pre-OPD samples each, two 100-step 1.5B–1.7B runs, pass@1/high-K and solution-route coverage. Estimated 40–90 A100-hours, 2–3 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** [On-Policy Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29495) makes plain replay a likely low-novelty baseline rather than a paper's central method.

### C04 — Matched-Delta Causal Control

**Claim.** Teacher-minus-base deltas may transfer generic policy shaping rather than acquired reasoning; a matched-ancestry factorial can separate the two.

**Method.** From one teacher base, construct reasoning-tuned and style-only descendants matched in data volume and update norm; distill each delta into identical students with matched prompts, seeds, and budgets; evaluate newly solvable problems, style, language, and sampler-envelope pass@K.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** Matched LoRA descendants around a 4B teacher, Qwen3-1.7B student, 50–100 steps, 300–10K math prompts. Estimated 24–160 A100-hours, 2–4 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** OPD² demonstrates gains from teacher-base probability gaps but does not, at abstract level, causally separate reasoning acquisition from other descendant-specific behavioral changes.

### C05 — Parallel-Language Gradient Intersection

**Claim.** The update component shared by aligned English/Korean/Japanese problems carries language-independent reasoning, while conflicting components drive surface-language drift.

**Method.** Compute per-language OPD² batch gradients on aligned problems and project onto a direction improving all views; compare ordinary multilingual and English-only OPD².

**Cheapest discriminative test.** English/Korean pilot, 10K aligned problems, Qwen3-1.7B, 100 steps. Estimated 60–120 A100-hours, 3–4 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** [Invariant Gradient Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05025) is a strong direct overlap; this idea survives only if on-policy state alignment and OPD² produce a materially different claim.

### C06 — Causal Update Grafting Across Objectives

**Claim.** OPD's small-K improvement and off-policy distillation's capability expansion are carried by separable parameter-update components.

**Method.** Train matched OPD and off-policy-distillation runs from one base; factor layerwise deltas; graft matched-norm low-rank components and cross-combinations onto fresh base copies; use random, sign-reversed, and norm-matched controls.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** One 1.5B model, 10K math examples, top low-rank attention/MLP delta components, pass@1/envelope pass@K and problem transitions. Estimated 60–120 A100-hours, 3–5 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** Local geometry and sparse-update papers already report structure; the novelty burden is functional causality under transplantation, not another cosine/SVD analysis.

### C07 — Modality-Null Counterfactual OPD Audit

**Claim.** Cross-modal OPD gains may reflect stronger language priors rather than improved perceptual grounding.

**Method.** Keep text fixed while using real, causally swapped, and blank images; train matched text-teacher OPD conditions; measure counterfactual answer sensitivity and text-solvable controls.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** A 2B VLM, 1K–2K controlled chart/geometry examples, three 50–100-step runs. Estimated 60–140 A100-hours, 2–4 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** VOLD, Decomposed OPD, X-OPD, and Video-OPD already claim cross-modal transfer; the audit must expose a result those standard benchmarks cannot distinguish.

### C08 — One-Step Gradient Causality Matrix

**Claim.** On the same student-generated states, teacher-matching gradients and verifier-outcome gradients can have opposite immediate causal effects even when their cosine appears benign.

**Method.** At early/mid/late checkpoints, compute OPD and verifier-based policy gradients on a fixed state batch; apply one matched-norm step to checkpoint clones plus sign-reversed/random controls; measure correct-continuation probability, next-batch rollout success, and teacher agreement.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** One 1.5B–1.7B student, 4B–7B teacher, 1K prompts, three checkpoints, eight verifier-scored rollouts. Estimated 25–60 A100-hours, 1–2 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** Reward-gated OPD and Distributional DAgger already combine or contrast outcome and teacher signals; novelty requires a causal measurement result, not another gating rule.

### C09 — Plan-Lattice OPD

**Claim.** OPD fails to acquire new reasoning because the student visits too narrow a strategy set; multiple student-generated high-level plans can widen visited states without using teacher-generated traces.

**Method.** Generate several distinct one-sentence plans per prompt, let the student complete one trajectory from each, and apply unchanged OPD over their union without teacher-based plan selection; equalize teacher-token budget.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** 500 math prompts, 1 versus 4 plans, two 100-step 1.5B runs; pass@1/pass@64, newly solved prompts, verified strategy coverage. Estimated 32–64 A100-hours, 2–3 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** State-distribution accounts and multi-rollout OPD already motivate broader visitation; the plan representation must create genuinely new state coverage beyond ordinary multi-sampling.

### C10 — Listwise Sequence-Distribution OPD

**Claim.** Matching the teacher's relative distribution over complete student trajectories can improve pass@1 without suppressing alternative solution modes as aggressively as token-local OPD.

**Method.** Sample groups of complete student trajectories, compute length-controlled teacher/student sequence scores, and fit the student's normalized distribution over each group; compare equal-budget token-level OPD and sequence-level controls.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** Four rollouts per prompt, 500 prompts, 100-step 1.5B runs. Estimated 48–96 A100-hours, 3–5 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** [Revisiting OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25562) already frames token-level OPD as a biased, lower-variance estimator relative to sequence-level reverse KL; listwise normalization needs a distinct variance/coverage result.

### C11 — Prompt-Orbit OPD

**Claim.** Coupling semantically equivalent prompts across languages and thinking modes can preserve requested surface modes while retaining shared reasoning gains.

**Method.** Build aligned language × thinking-mode prompt orbits, generate one on-policy trajectory per view, enforce final-answer consistency and mode/language compliance, and report joint—not accuracy-only—success.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** 300 English/Korean/Japanese math problems × two modes, Qwen3-1.7B, two 50-step runs. Estimated 20–50 A100-hours, 2–4 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** OPD² has already observed cross-language accuracy gains with English drift; novelty depends on showing that orbit coupling solves an identified failure instead of simply adding consistency regularization.

### C12 — Amortized Compute Frontier for OPD

**Claim.** Whether OPD is preferable to spending the same end-to-end compute on more base-model test-time samples depends on deployment query volume, a break-even ordinary equal-K comparisons hide.

**Method.** Measure rollout, teacher scoring, backward, and inference costs; allocate one budget to OPD-training-plus-inference or unchanged-base inference scaling; plot accuracy, latency, coverage, and break-even volume with and without a verifier.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** Reuse one 1.5B base/OPD pair and logs, 500 prompts to K=64. Estimated 2–10 A100-hours, 3–7 days.

**Closest pressure.** Recent train/test compute-allocation papers already optimize deployment-matched objectives; novelty must be an OPD-specific reversal or scaling law, not bookkeeping.

### C13 — Fresh-State Rehearsal for Continual OPD

**Claim.** Sequential-OPD forgetting is partly loss of visitation to old-task prefix states; storing only historical prompts and regenerating current-policy states should outperform answer replay.

**Method.** Keep a small prompt reservoir, generate fresh current-student rollouts on old and new tasks, and apply one fixed teacher's ordinary OPD supervision; compare sequential OPD, answer-level SFT rehearsal, and equal-budget joint training.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** Qwen3-1.7B, three domains, 50–100 steps each, 256 prompts retained per domain; pass@1/pass@64 after every stage. Estimated 60–140 H100-hours, 2–4 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** [On-Policy Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29495) is extremely close in data-source logic; the teacher-scored on-policy-state distinction may be incremental.

### C14 — Equation-State Coupled Multilingual OPD

**Claim.** Aligning hidden states at language-independent mathematical events can transfer reusable reasoning without shifting Korean/Japanese outputs into English.

**Method.** Parse matched equation/operator events in parallel-language on-policy rollouts; add hidden-state agreement at those events while freezing embeddings and LM head; compare multilingual OPD and alignment/freezing ablations.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** Qwen3-1.7B, 10K aligned problems, 50–100 steps, PolyMath/Global-MGSM, pass@1 and larger pass@K. Estimated 120–240 H100-hours, 3–5 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** On-Policy Representation Distillation and invariant-gradient methods already use internal alignment; a parser-defined event unit may not be enough novelty by itself.

### C15 — Checkpoint-Invariant Delta-Subspace Distillation

**Claim.** The component of teacher change stable across base, instruction-tuned, and reasoning-post-trained checkpoints is more reusable than checkpoint-specific residuals.

**Method.** Score identical current-student states with a compatible teacher checkpoint chain; compute layerwise adjacent activation deltas; estimate a shared low-rank subspace; distill shared and residual components into matched zero-initialized adapters.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** One 1.7B student, 2–3 compatible open teacher checkpoints, 5K mixed math/code prompts, one adapter rank, 50 steps per condition. Estimated 180–360 H100-hours, 4–6 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** OPD², representation distillation, geometry work, and temporally adaptive teachers each cover a component; the idea risks being an overbuilt intersection without a unique theorem or result.

### C16 — OPD Adapter Transplantation and Composition

**Claim.** If OPD acquires reusable skills rather than only sharpening source-task sampling, equal-rank adapters should transfer or compose across related tasks and languages without further optimization.

**Method.** Freeze a common backbone; train separate equal-rank OPD adapters on math, code, and multilingual math; cross-evaluate every adapter; compose pairs by fixed addition and compare with a rank-matched jointly trained adapter plus random controls.

**Cheapest discriminative test.** Three rank-16 adapters on Qwen3-1.7B, 50–100 steps each, all single/pairwise compositions at pass@1/pass@64. Estimated 80–180 H100-hours, 2–4 weeks.

**Closest pressure.** Task-vector and adapter-merging work make composition unsurprising in general; novelty must come from an OPD-specific functional separation between sampling efficiency and capability acquisition.
