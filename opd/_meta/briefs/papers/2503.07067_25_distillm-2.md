<h6 class="ltx_title ltx_title_abstract">Abstract</h6>
<p class="ltx_p" id="id4.id1">Despite the success of distillation in large language models (LLMs), most prior work applies identical loss functions to both teacher- and student-generated data. These strategies overlook the synergy between loss formulations and data types, leading to a suboptimal performance boost in student models. To address this, we propose <span class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold ltx_font_smallcaps" id="id4.id1.1">DistiLLM-2</span>, a contrastive approach that simultaneously increases the likelihood of teacher responses and decreases that of student responses by harnessing this synergy. Our extensive experiments show that <span class="ltx_text ltx_font_smallcaps" id="id4.id1.2">DistiLLM-2</span> not only builds high-performing student models across a wide range of tasks, including instruction-following and code generation, but also supports diverse applications, such as preference alignment and vision-language extensions. These findings highlight the potential of a contrastive approach to enhance the efficacy of LLM distillation by effectively aligning teacher and student models across varied data types.</p>
