<h5 class="ltx_title ltx_title_paragraph">Learning from Experience</h5>
<div id="S5.SS0.SSS0.Px3.p1" class="ltx_para">
<p id="S5.SS0.SSS0.Px3.p1.1" class="ltx_p">Learning from experience has long been a central theme in artificial intelligence. A recent position paper argues that agents should primarily learn from their own interaction with the world rather than from human-curated data, heralding an era of experience <cite class="ltx_cite ltx_citemacro_citep">[<a href="#bib.bib4" title="Welcome to the era of experience" class="ltx_ref">13</a>]</cite>. Along this direction, early-stage interaction experience has been shown to accelerate agent learning in subsequent tasks <cite class="ltx_cite ltx_citemacro_citep">[<a href="#bib.bib3" title="Agent learning via early experience" class="ltx_ref">19</a>]</cite>, and reasoning-based agents have demonstrated the ability to discover game strategies through self-play and reflection <cite class="ltx_cite ltx_citemacro_citep">[<a href="#bib.bib17" title="Cogito, ergo ludo: an agent that learns to play by reasoning and planning" class="ltx_ref">15</a>]</cite>. In the language model community, several methods have explored leveraging interaction histories: <cite class="ltx_cite ltx_citemacro_citep">[<a href="#bib.bib1" title="Reflexion: language agents with verbal reinforcement learning" class="ltx_ref">12</a>]</cite> prompts models to reflect on past failures to guide future attempts, while <cite class="ltx_cite ltx_citemacro_citep">[<a href="#bib.bib2" title="Expel: llm agents are experiential learners" class="ltx_ref">20</a>]</cite> extracts insights from trajectories and stores them in external memory for retrieval.</p>
