Stage-1 Controls the Entropy Regime, Not the Outcome
On-Policy Distillation versus SFT as a Warm-Start for Small-Data VLM RL
Jianxiong Shen
Abstract
Two-stage post-training — a Stage-1 warm-start (supervised fine-tuning, SFT, or on-policy distillation, OPD) followed by Stage-2 reinforcement learning (RL) — is increasingly used for vision-language models (VLMs). We ask what Stage-1 actually controls in a small-data study using Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a same-modality 72B VLM teacher for OPD. First, the three warm-starts reach a narrow 53 53 – 54 % 54\% band on Geometry3K internal validation, consistent with the narrow range reported by recent specialized methods; this setup provides little evidence that Stage-1 changes the in-domain endpoint. Second, a matched-recipe, early-stopped SFT improves out-of-domain MathVista by + 2.1 +2.1 points, reversing the − 9.5 -9.5 -point drop of an over-trained variant. The clearest difference is the entropy regime : OPD enters RL with substantially higher policy entropy than either SFT initialization, and the separation remains visible through the available trajectories. At the in-domain initialization, OPD also has higher answer diversity and pass@16 ( + 2.0 +2.0 to + 5.2 +5.2 points over SFT), although problem-level bootstrap intervals show that the smaller contrast is uncertain. The advantage is absent after RL (endpoint pass@16 values within 1.1 1.1 points) and on MathVista (six models within 1.2 1.2 points). Our contribution is therefore a bounded empirical characterization: Stage-1 is strongly associated with the entropy regime in this setup, but the downstream payoff is small, localized, and not evidence that OPD is a better RL warm-start.

1 Introduction
Post-training of vision-language models increasingly follows a two-stage template: a Stage-1 warm-start that injects task structure, then Stage-2 RL (typically GRPO-style) that optimizes a verifiable reward. For the warm-start, two families dominate — supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on curated traces, and on-policy distillation (OPD), which trains the student on its own rollouts under a teacher’s token distribution. The choice is often treated as important and is defended by two recurring arguments: that SFT induces catastrophic forgetting of base capabilities, and that one warm-start yields a better initialization for RL.
We interrogate these claims in the small-data regime ( ≈ 1 \approx 1 – 2 2 k examples) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B [ 1 ] . Our OPD uses a same-modality teacher — a 72B Qwen2.5-VL — distinguishing it from cross-modal (text-teacher) distillation. We compare three warm-starts followed by closely matched GRPO recipes, evaluated in-domain (Geometry3K) and out-of-domain (MathVista [ 4 ] ). Our findings are:
(F1) The observed forgetting is recipe-dependent. A matched-recipe, early-stopped SFT improves OOD MathVista by + 2.1 +2.1 over base; the − 9.5 -9.5 drop appears only in the longer, prompt-mismatched variant.
(F2) In-domain endpoints occupy a narrow band. The three warm-starts reach 53 53 – 54 % 54\% best internal validation, similar to the range reported by recent specialized methods. This is evidence of limited headroom in this setup, not a universal saturation claim.
(F3, core) Stage-1 is associated with distinct entropy regimes, with bounded payoff. OPD trajectories have substantially higher entropy than the two SFT trajectories. The corresponding pass@ k k advantage appears only at the in-domain initialization and is absent after RL and OOD.
The contribution is not a method or a leaderboard number but a characterization : a clean separation of what Stage-1 does and does not control, with the entropy regime identified as the single reliable axis and its consequences honestly bounded.
