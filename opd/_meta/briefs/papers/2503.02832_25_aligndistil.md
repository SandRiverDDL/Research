<h6 class="ltx_title ltx_title_abstract">Abstract</h6>
<p class="ltx_p" id="id10.id1">In modern large language models (LLMs), LLM alignment is of crucial importance and is typically achieved through methods such as reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and direct preference optimization (DPO).
However, in most existing methods for LLM alignment, all tokens in the response are optimized using a sparse, response-level reward or preference annotation.
The ignorance of token-level rewards may erroneously punish high-quality tokens or encourage low-quality tokens, resulting in suboptimal performance and slow convergence speed.
To address this issue, we propose <em class="ltx_emph ltx_font_bold ltx_font_italic" id="id10.id1.1">AlignDistil</em>, an RLHF-equivalent distillation method for token-level reward optimization.
Specifically, we introduce the reward learned by DPO into the RLHF objective and theoretically prove the equivalence between this objective and a token-level distillation process, where the teacher distribution linearly combines the logits from the DPO model and a reference model.
On this basis, we further bridge the accuracy gap between the reward from the DPO model and the pure reward model, by building a contrastive DPO reward with a normal and a reverse DPO model.
Moreover, to avoid under- and over-optimization on different tokens, we design a token adaptive logit extrapolation mechanism to construct an appropriate teacher distribution for each token.
Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our AlignDistil over existing methods and showcase fast convergence due to its token-level distributional reward optimization.
</p>

## [limitations] offset 29044 pattern `\blimitation`

Yoon et al., <a class="ltx_ref" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02832v1#bib.bib48" title="">2024</a>; Li et al., <a class="ltx_ref" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02832v1#bib.bib24" title="">2024c</a>; Xia et al., <a class="ltx_ref" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02832v1#bib.bib44" title="">2024</a>; Yang et al., <a class="ltx_ref" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02832v1#bib.bib47" title="">2024c</a>)</cite>, which may erroneously punish tokens with high quality or encourage tokens with low quality.

<div class="ltx_para" id="S1.p3">
<p class="ltx_p" id="S1.p3.1">To address this issue, in this paper, we propose <span class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold ltx_font_italic" id="S1.p3.1.1">AlignDistil</span> (as shown in Figure <a class="ltx_ref" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02832v1#S0.F1" title="Figure 1 ‣ AlignDistil: Token-Level Language Model Alignment as Adaptive Policy Distillation"><span class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">1</span></a>), a simple distillation method derived from the RLHF objective for token-level reward optimization.
Specifically, our method starts from introducing the DPO reward <cite class="ltx_cite ltx_citemacro_citep">(Rafailov et al., <a class="ltx_ref" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02832v1#bib.bib35" title="">2024b</a>)</cite> into the original objective of RLHF.
Based on the property of token-level decomposition of the DPO reward <cite class="ltx_cite ltx_citemacro_citep">(Rafailov et al., <a class="ltx_ref" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02832v1#bib.bib34" title="">2024a</a>)</cite>, we prove a theoretical equivalence between the original sequence-level objective of RLHF and a token-level distillation objective.
In this distillation objective, the current policy is guided by a teacher distribution that linearly combines the logit distribution output from the two LLMs in the DPO reward.
Built on this theoretical finding, our AlignDistil further involves two targeted designs for token-level optimization.
