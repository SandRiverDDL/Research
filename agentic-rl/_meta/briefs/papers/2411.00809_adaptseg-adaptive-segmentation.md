[p.1] Adaptive Segment-level Reward: Bridging the Gap Between Action and Reward Space in Alignment Yanshi Li1, Shaopan Xiong1, Gengru Chen1,2, Xiaoyang Li1, Yijia Luo1, Xingyuan Bu1, Yingshui Tan1, Wenbo Su 1, Bo Zheng1, 1Alibaba Group, 2Tsinghua University {liyanshi.lys, xiongshaopan.xsp, vincent.swb}@alibaba-inc.com Abstract Reinforcement Learning (RL) has proven highly effective in aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences. Typi- cal RL methods optimize under an overall se- quence reward, which can lead to a suboptimal learning process. This reflects a key credit as- signment problem: identifying which tokens to reinforce or suppress. To rectify these short- comings, step-wise and token-wise methods have been proposed. However, step-wise meth- ods rely on punctuation segmentation and still cannot accurately identify the key tokens. The token-level approach is too fine-grained, attend- ing to many unimportant tokens and thus intro- ducing a large amount of noise. To assign more accurate rewards to different tokens, improving credit assignment, we propose the "Adaptive Segment-wise Reward" method. We employ semantic meaning, rather than punctuation, to adaptively delineate segments. Experiments demonstrate that our method can be integrated into various training methods. Therefore, this paper proposes an "Adaptive Segment-level Reward" method to address the aforementioned problems. The core of this method is to segment the text based on semantic informa- tion, thereby avoiding the issues where step-level rewards fail to find key tokens and token-level re- wards have a low signal-to-noise ratio. First, we segment the text using token rewards. We identify "pivot tokens" as those where the reward changes significantly compared to the reward of the previ- ous token. These pivot tokens are used to define segments. Token rewards can be given by reward models, rule-based rewards, or annotators. Next, we compare the reward of each segment with the overall reward of the entire sequence. This allows us to identify segments that have rewards aligned with (the same sign as) and opposed to (the oppo- site sign of) the overall sequence reward. Finally, during training, we only backpropagate through segments whose reward signs are consistent with the overall reward of the entire sequence. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method through adversarial evaluations and open-source benchmark evaluations. In adversarial evaluations against the base model, our method achieves an 86% win rate, an improvement of over 10% com- pared to the baseline, as shown in Figure 4 and Figure 5. On open-source benchmarks, we achieve an average improvement of 1.3%, as presented in Section 4 and Appendix F. Furthermore, this paper theoretically analyzes the gap between the action space and the reward space in existing alignment methods. The analysis reveals the source of this gap and provides a unified error formula. Based on our theoretical analysis, our method is superior to both step-level and token- level approaches. The contributions of this paper are as follows: • This paper proposes a novel method for op- timizing rewards in preference alignment: "Adaptive Segment-level Reward". • We propose removing tokens that contra- dict the sequence-level reward signal. This achieves performance comparable to or bet- ter than step-wise methods, while maintaining the same computational cost as sequence-wise training methods. • We reveal why finer-grained reward signals are necessary in large language model align- ment and provide a theoretical unified error formula. 2
